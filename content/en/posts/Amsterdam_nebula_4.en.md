**Everything is negotiable, except the kaascroissant (cheese croissant).**

I am not one of those who believe in conspiracy theories.

On a beautiful summer morning, the sun seemed happy in Amsterdam, looking down upon our faces for the first time in a year.

My feet took me where they wanted to take me every morning.

I put on my headphones and listened to a Chinese song I had recently discovered. It was released in 1993 and was called *Water Town in My Dreams*—in Chinese: 梦里水乡.

Of course, my subconscious followed its well-worn algorithm:

To Albert Heijn, and straight to that little square glass display where the kaascroissant lie.

They looked beautiful, as they do every day. That little square of glass seemed like a tiny garden to me inside the supermarket.

Until my eyes collided with the price.

1.

They write it as 0.99.

There is no difference between these two numbers—not even in the weights of large language models.

My romantic morning shattered when my eyes reached the cheese croissant. The one with added meat—ham—was still at its old price.

Only the cheese croissant.

I felt a revolutionary spirit rising in me. In protest, I walked out without buying anything. I headed toward the competitors, determined to make my purchase there as my greater act of protest.

I arrived at Lidl.

All the bread baskets were full, except the one for kaascroissant.

I thought perhaps they simply hadn't baked them yet.

I went to another one, Then another...

I took a bus that cost €3.

And in every supermarket, the kaascroissant basket stood there like an orphan.

What was happening?

Had the cows rebelled?

But the cheese croissant with added meat was still at the same price.

I returned to Albert Heijn to buy it.

Then I remembered how addiction makes a person submissive.

When I arrived, they were all gone.

Sold out.

This was the workers' food.

All the workers, with their white, red, green, brown, and every other kind of collar.

For months, in the squares of Amsterdam, I had watched people protesting.

Others handed out protest leaflets.

Others distributed religious posters.

And every time, my heart secretly wished to see a poster with a kaascroissant on it.

**Everything is negotiable, except the kaascroissant.**

You may believe that the Earth is flat, 
that nobody went to the Moon,
 that the sun does not exist in winter,
  and that cows do not fart...

But no one believes that there is a conspiracy to cancel the kaascroissant from labour diet.

Just the cheese croissant.
