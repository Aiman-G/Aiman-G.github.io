The Amsterdam Nebula 7

My Albert Hijn ?
2 min

After arriving in the Netherlands, like every stranger, I had a lot of questions, among them questions about where to get things. My Dutch friend used to tell me,

“Go to your Albert Heijn. You might find it there.”

At the beginning, it took my brain a second to process this sentence. After all, my English is not as good as the average Dutch person’s.

“Your Albert Heijn.”

This is not a normal sentence to my brain.

It is my Albert Heijn. Everyone has their own Albert Heijn. I am here, after weeks, new to this country, and I have mijn.

But this “your”—does it mean your, as my brain understood it?

Or does it mean that I belong to that Albert Heijn?

Both meanings have that thread that gives a stranger a sense of belonging.

Day after day, when I go to other places and look at their Albert Heijns, I start to compare them to my Albert Heijn.

My Albert Heijn is in Geldrop, and there is another Albert Heijn in the city center, less than 70 meters away. At their Albert Heijn, they have chicken rice, a delicious ready meal that my Albert Heijn doesn’t have.

That hurts me.

When I buy that delicious rice, I feel like someone is watching me over my shoulder. I quickly enter, pick up my rice, pay at the automatic checkout, and leave like a thief. I hurry out before someone from my Albert Heijn sees me cheating on my Albert Heijn.

At my Albert Heijn, I know every face. I compare them to the people at other Albert Heijns. The girls at my Albert Heijn look like models, and the grandmothers are like my aunts—sweet and wise.

It is my Albert Heijn.

In Amsterdam, near Central Station, there is an Albert Heijn in a historic building.

You won’t believe your eyes when you enter that place; it’s as if you’re entering a museum.

You can smell the history, the stones, and the bread.

The good thing is that the interior is not as wide and open as my Albert Heijn. Only two people can barely walk inside. That gives me some solace.

When I open my ING app, I see:

  • −AH
  • −AH
  • −AH

You know that a negative AH followed by some number, looks like a blood type.
